With 5 bp one-way trading costs, a 0.20% annual roll-cost proxy on gross notional and full cash credit, the 2001-03-28–2024-03-28 baseline has CAGR of **9.39%** for futures and **7.09%** for equities. The two 2019 gap exits book estimated P&L of **+$211.95** and **−$593.04**, plus exit costs of **$52.02** and **$144.68**. These fills and intervening rolls remain unverified. The quote/session issue and other audit limitations are not resolved. The findings, figures and sensitivities below document the **original 2026-11-22 notebook**, not a rerun of every scenario after these changes.

**Decision: revise before publishing a performance conclusion.** The saved result reproduces: the simulated futures strategy earned **10.51% CAGR** versus **6.40%** for the equity proxy. The breakout does not appear to trade on the price that triggered it. The consequential problems are elsewhere: the comparison starts before the futures portfolio can take risk; having a dated mark is treated as permission to trade; two live holdings disappear at a data break; and the return ranking is exceptionally sensitive to how much trading costs. The evidence supports an illustrative historical simulation, not a claim of unbiased, implementable, or reliably superior performance.

## 2. What the data establish, and what they do not

The main evidence is stronger than a visual spot-check: all **30** adjusted/active files in the study universe were compared to their pinned upstream files after taking the last nonmissing observation per source date. No dates differed; the largest absolute price discrepancy was below **0.000000000034** quote units. Across those 30 files plus DFF, EUR/USD and MSCI Net, **298,656** cached rows passed ordering, uniqueness and finite-value checks. URTH is an auxiliary comparison, not one of these 33 main inputs. This establishes extraction fidelity to one vintage, **not** accuracy of the original quotes, tradeability, point-in-time availability, or rights to republish them.

**Source chain checked.** The downloader reads PST `adjusted_prices_csv/price` and `multiple_prices_csv/PRICE` at the pinned commit; it does not splice the separate legacy or Yahoo histories into the trading panel. Thus the clean CSVs cannot distinguish all raw omissions, intraday timestamps, or vendor revisions. Retain original contract IDs, timestamps and raw missing-row counts for roll/session verification. The three FX futures already have USD point values. 82 FESX and 144 Bund marks lacked a same-day FRED fix; the prior fix was never more than three days old. ## 3. Strategy, timing and bias

**What passes.** The guide's combined rule is recognizable: 50/100 recursive EMA direction filter for entries, 100-close entry and 50-close exit channels, symmetric breakouts, flat between trades, no instant reversal. The notebook uses next **close**, not next open. The breakout windows exclude today's close. A signal at close *t* sets direction for the next observed close; the old holding earns the move into that fill; the new holding earns only subsequent moves. Contract risk and benchmark risk are shifted, the basket window stops before the fill, and sizing uses prior NAV. A toy close path `10,11,12,13,11,10,9,8,9,10` (spans 2/3, channels 2/1) yielded post-close directions `0,0,0,1,1,0,−1,−1,−1,0`; subtracting 100 from all prices did not change them. Rerunning on inputs cut at **2018-06-29** reproduced both baseline NAV prefixes exactly. These checks rule out specific code-level leaks, not vendor revisions or hindsight in research design.

A future constant Panama offset on an earlier price prefix shifts both MAs and both sides of a channel comparison together; it disappears from point differences. **Back-adjustment alone is not evidence of look-ahead in this rule.** Historical corrections, hindsight in contract selection, and calendar/missing-mark knowledge are different questions. Likewise, equal *standalone* risk before scaling is not equal sector risk or equal marginal contribution to portfolio risk. Volatility scaling and cross-asset trend are established research approaches (see [Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003)); their findings do not validate this particular universe, filter, or execution model.

## 4. How easily the result changes

Single changes below leave all other baseline inputs fixed, preserve the same dates, and compound their own NAV. These are diagnostics, **not** a search for the best setting. The 2× diagnostic scales all target quantities proportionately based on prior-close estimated gross notional before filling; it is not a hard intraday limit. Annual roll drag is charged on gross notional, not per roll. Neither illustrative cost scenario is a verified estimate of actual friction.

| Case | Futures CAGR | Excess Sharpe | Volatility | Max drawdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline: 5 bp one way | 10.51% | 0.57 | 16.91% | −35.84% |
| No trading cost | 14.75% | 0.79 | 16.90% | −33.66% |
| 10 bp one way | **6.43%** | **0.35** | 16.95% | −37.96% |
| 25 bp annual roll drag | 9.08% | 0.49 | 16.91% | −36.42% |
| No cash interest | 8.70% | 0.47 | 16.91% | −35.90% |
| Weekly resizing; daily entries/exits | 11.33% | 0.61 | 16.98% | −34.22% |
| Risk target 0.75× benchmark | 8.58% | 0.57 | 12.68% | −27.85% |
| Approximate 2× gross notional cap | 6.15% | 0.55 | 8.34% | −17.28% |
| Nearest whole contract at each target | 10.77% | 0.57 | 17.32% | −35.92% |
| 10 bp trading + 25 bp annual roll + 75% cash yield | **4.61%** | **0.25** | 16.95% | −41.04% |
| Equity proxy (unchanged) | **6.40%** | **0.35** | 16.29% | −57.87% |

At **10 bp** the annualized return advantage essentially vanishes without changing signals or realized volatility materially. Whole-contract rounding in this one replay slightly *helps* returns; it is not evidence of margin feasibility. Delaying just direction changes by one additional observed close gives **9.92%** CAGR. Roll spread/carry is not a fee: adjusted differences are intended to capture holding P&L, while spreads, slippage and commissions on actual roll trades are a separate omission. Do not deduct the price gap between expiries as an extra cash loss.

**Is it trend, or simply owning a different basket?** With the same markets, warm-up, lag, risk model, collateral and 5 bp costs, an always-long eligible basket earns **8.81% CAGR** (Sharpe 0.48, drawdown −57.29%); always-in EMA direction earns **9.36%** (0.51, −35.24%); unfiltered breakout earns **9.80%** (0.53, −28.28%). The hybrid's 10.51% is above all three here, but its drawdown is *worse* than breakout alone. These are controls within the same flawed calendar and same sample, not out-of-sample evidence.

**Universe controls.** Allow only the eleven markets already eligible at the first shared date, retaining their subsequent gap rules: futures CAGR **10.99%** versus the baseline 10.51%. Remove one sector at a time, leaving the sizing system to reallocate risk: CAGR is **10.09%** without equities, **10.21%** without bonds, **9.42%** without FX, **8.23%** without energy, **10.01%** without metals and **10.06%** without agriculture. No single sector’s removal reversed the full-period ranking at 5 bp. Energy mattered most in this test; these are portfolio counterfactuals, not additive sector contributions. Neither test recovers missing or discontinued markets.

**Regime and uncertainty.** Rebase the existing portfolios, without resetting their holdings, and CAGR changes: from the **2001-03-29** first risk-ready close, **10.57% vs 7.13%**; from **2010-01-04**, **8.89% vs 9.69%**; from **2015-08-13**, when the last instrument first qualifies, **9.96% vs 10.00%**. In calendar 2008 the strategies returned +76.12% and −40.77%; in 2009, −15.96% and +29.86%. Those are economically meaningful episodes, not independent replications. An illustrative paired circular-block bootstrap of daily log-return differences (63-observation blocks, 5,000 draws, annualized using the observed ~257.7 paired observations/year) gives a 95% percentile interval of approximately **−7.5 to +15.7 annual log-percentage points**, around an observed **+3.80**. It includes zero. This measures uncertainty conditional on the selected sample and dependence model; it does not correct data mining or validate a future return forecast.

## 5. Edits and checks before publication

1. **Fix the comparison and ledger first.** Define the common start based on all required lagged risk inputs; align exchange sessions and audit the Christmas trades; preserve live holdings across missing prices. Recompute every chart and table, including the above sensitivity results after these changes. If timestamps or fills cannot be recovered, use a deliberately coarser verified sampling convention or narrow the claims.
2. **Release an assumptions table alongside performance.** Specify USD NAV, variation-margin FX convention, collateral return, execution delay, eligible contracts/rolls, gross leverage, turnover, fractionality and cost units. Show futures P&L before costs, trading/roll costs, cash interest and total return separately. Equal gross notional need not mean equal risk, especially in short-duration bonds.
3. **Make selection and robustness visible.** Publish an eligibility timeline, runner-up/failed candidate criteria, fixed-universe and leave-one-sector-out results, period splits, and the same-universe controls. Include the fee-free MSCI index and the URTH overlap. Do not pick a new winner from the sliders and present it as the original hypothesis.
4. **Tighten descriptions and plots.** Rename “MSCI World ETF” everywhere to “MSCI World Net USD less assumed 10 bp”; identify ZS and CL expiry conventions, and distinguish available/eligible/open markets. The raw-level diagnostic truncates the sample at 2024-01-01 and overlays incompatible units without a visible legend; use per-instrument panels (including the final quarter), point-change/offset plots and flagged dates. Display the gap and roll exception tables rather than merely constructing them. Call the absolute-move alternative a close-move estimator, not ATR. Do not imply the fixed 1% cash parameter or risk cap knobs are functional. Rename the transaction-cost constant as a decimal rate, or store basis points and convert once (the current 0.0005 really is 5 bp). Clear the direction cache when editing inputs in a live kernel; it is keyed by signal parameters, not data or eligibility.
5. **Describe metrics precisely.** Rolling five-year returns are cumulative; the rolling correlation is 252 paired returns, not exactly a calendar year. CAGR uses elapsed time; volatility/Sharpe/Sortino assume 252 periods/year despite ~258 paired marks/year. Mean drawdown duration weights many small episodes equally and includes an unfinished one at its current age; add longest drawdown and time underwater. Use a consistent risk-free hurdle for both portfolios, including when varying the futures cash credit. Show uncertainty, not just point estimates.
6. **Make the study reproducible without redistributing restricted data.** Freeze the notebook, environment, manifest and acquisition instructions. The futures commit is pinned; FRED, MSCI and Yahoo snapshots are not immutable upstream. Local data are ignored by Git. Check provider terms for the proposed **charts, tables and derived outputs**, not just raw-CSV redistribution; retain attribution. Do not call a public download a redistribution license. **Defensible conclusion today:** on one selected set of provider futures chains, a lagged trend rule produced a higher simulated return and smaller worst drawdown than a hypothetical global equity fund over this interval. The result is sensitive to calendar integrity, start date, cost assumptions and market selection. It does not establish that the rule outperforms a global equity portfolio at equal risk or that the historical returns were attainable.
